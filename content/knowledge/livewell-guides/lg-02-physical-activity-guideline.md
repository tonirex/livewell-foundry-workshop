# How Much Physical Activity Do Adults Need?

Regular movement supports heart health, mood, sleep, strength, and daily energy. Start from your current level and build up in a way that feels safe and repeatable.

## The weekly target

Adults can aim for 150-300 minutes of moderate-intensity activity a week, or 75-150 minutes of vigorous-intensity activity a week. You can also mix moderate and vigorous activity across the week.

MVPA = moderate-to-vigorous physical activity minutes.

Add muscle-strengthening activity on 2+ days each week. This can include bodyweight exercises, resistance bands, carrying groceries, stair climbing, or supervised gym work. Also reduce long sitting by standing, stretching, or walking for a few minutes whenever you have been seated for a long time.

| Activity type | What it feels like | Examples |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moderate | Breathing faster but still in control | Brisk walking, cycling on flat ground |
| Vigorous | Harder breathing and more effort | Jogging, fast cycling, sports drills |
| Strength | Muscles working against resistance | Squats, wall push-ups, bands |

## Use the talk test

The talk test is a simple intensity check. During moderate activity, you can talk in short sentences but cannot sing comfortably. During vigorous activity, you can only say a few words before needing to breathe. If you cannot speak at all, slow down.

## Steps are a useful everyday proxy

Step counts are not perfect, but they help many people notice patterns. If you are not active now, add a small amount first, such as 500-1,000 extra steps a day for one or two weeks. Over time, many adults can build gradually towards about 7,000-10,000 steps a day, depending on health, work, and mobility.

Do not worry if your step count is lower on some days. Look at the weekly pattern. A day with a brisk 20-minute walk, housework, and stairs still counts as progress.

## Make activity fit normal life

Try walking to a further bus stop, taking stairs for one or two floors, doing a short home workout, or joining a community class. Break activity into smaller blocks if that is easier. Ten minutes after meals, three times a day, can be a realistic start.

## Key points

- Aim for 150-300 minutes of moderate activity, or 75-150 minutes of vigorous activity, each week.
- Include muscle-strengthening activity on 2+ days each week.
- Use the talk test to check intensity.
- Reduce long sitting with short movement breaks.
- Build steps gradually instead of jumping to a high target overnight.

## When to talk to a health professional

Check with a doctor before starting if you have a health condition, chest discomfort, unexplained breathlessness, dizziness, recent surgery, or you are unsure what level is safe.
