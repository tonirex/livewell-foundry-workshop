# Health Screening: What and When

Health screening helps find risk factors early, before symptoms appear. Your doctor will advise what to screen for and how often, based on age, results, family history, and personal risk.

## Common adult screening topics

The schedule below is general workshop guidance, not a personal plan. Some people need earlier or more frequent checks.

| Screening topic | What it checks | Typical frequency language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blood pressure | Strain on blood vessels and heart | Every 1-3 years depending on results and age |
| Blood glucose | Diabetes or higher-risk glucose levels | Every 1-3 years depending on results and age |
| Cholesterol | Blood fats linked with heart risk | Every 1-3 years depending on results and age |
| BMI and waist | Weight-related risk | At routine health checks |
| Certain cancer screenings | Early signs in selected groups | Your doctor will advise |

Screening is useful only if you understand the result and follow up. Keep a copy of your results and note the date, because trends over time can matter.

## What a High-risk result means

A High-risk result does not mean panic. It means you should follow up with a health professional to confirm what the result means, whether more checks are needed, and what lifestyle changes are suitable. Do not ignore it because you feel well.

If the result was taken at a roadshow or temporary station, arrange follow-up through a clinic. Bring your report and tell the doctor about any symptoms, family history, and usual routines.

## Prepare for screening

Before your appointment, check whether you need to avoid food before the test. Bring identification, past results if you have them, and a list of current health conditions. Wear clothing that makes blood pressure and waist measurement easy.

After screening, ask three questions: What does my result mean? What should I do next? When should I repeat the check?

## Keep the habit simple

Link screening to a memorable time of year, such as your birthday month. If your doctor gives a shorter follow-up interval, follow that instead of waiting for the next routine check.

## Key points

- Screening can find risk factors before symptoms appear.
- Common topics include blood pressure, blood glucose, cholesterol, BMI, waist, and certain cancer screenings.
- Many checks are repeated every 1-3 years depending on results and age; your doctor will advise.
- Follow up after a High-risk result even if you feel well.
- Keep results so you can track changes over time.

## When to talk to a health professional

Speak to a doctor if you have a High-risk result, symptoms, strong family history, pregnancy, or you are unsure which screening applies to you.
