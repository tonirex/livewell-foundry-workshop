# Sleep Well

Good sleep supports mood, focus, appetite, and energy for healthy living. Most adults do best with 7-9 hours of sleep, along with a routine that helps the body wind down.

## Set a steady rhythm

Try to wake up at about the same time each day, even after a late night. Morning light, breakfast, and movement can help set your body clock. At night, keep a short wind-down routine: shower, prepare clothes for tomorrow, dim the lights, and do a quiet activity.

| Habit | Try this | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Wake time | Keep it steady | Anchors the body clock |
| Wind-down | 20-30 quiet minutes | Signals that sleep is coming |
| Bedroom | Cool, dark, and calm | Reduces disturbance |
| Naps | Short and early | Protects night sleep |

## Screens and alerts

Phones and laptops can keep the brain busy. If possible, stop work messages, gaming, and scrolling at least 30 minutes before bed. Put the phone away from the bed, switch off non-urgent alerts, and use a simple alarm clock if the phone tempts you to keep checking.

If you need to use a screen at night, lower the brightness and choose calm content. Avoid difficult conversations and stressful tasks close to bedtime where you can.

## Caffeine, meals, and activity

Caffeine can stay active for hours. If sleep is poor, keep coffee, strong tea, and energy drinks to the morning or early afternoon. Heavy meals and alcohol close to bedtime can also disturb sleep. Gentle stretching or a slow walk in the evening is fine for many people, but hard exercise late at night may make it harder to settle.

## When sleep is interrupted

If you wake up and cannot sleep, keep the lights low. Try breathing slowly or reading something quiet. If you are still awake after a while, get out of bed briefly and return when sleepy. This helps the bed stay linked with sleep rather than frustration.

Track sleep for one to two weeks if you are trying to improve it. Note bedtime, wake time, caffeine timing, naps, and how refreshed you feel.

## Key points

- Most adults need 7-9 hours of sleep.
- Keep wake time steady and build a short wind-down routine.
- Reduce screens, alerts, and stressful tasks before bed.
- Move caffeine earlier if sleep is poor.
- Seek help if sleep problems persist or affect daily life.

## When to talk to a health professional

Speak to a doctor if poor sleep lasts more than a few weeks, you snore loudly with choking or pauses, feel very sleepy in the day, have low mood, or sleep problems affect work, driving, or relationships.
