# Staying Active on Hazy Days

Hazy weather does not mean you must stop moving completely, but it may change where and how hard you exercise. Plan indoor or lower-intensity options before you need them.

LiveWell treats a region as hazy when the regional 24-hour PSI is 55 or above. This is a regional context signal, not a measure of your personal exposure.

## Check before heading out

Check the PSI before outdoor activity, especially before long runs, sports sessions, or brisk walks. Conditions can differ by region: Central, East, North, North-East, and West may not always be the same. If the air quality is poor, reduce prolonged strenuous outdoor activity and choose a safer setting.

| Situation | Better option | Note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Planned outdoor run | Indoor walk or class | Keep effort moderate |
| Lunch walk | Mall walking | Avoid crowded fast pacing |
| Home evening | Short bodyweight routine | Keep windows closed if needed |
| Void deck or stairs | Gentle routine with caution | Watch footing and ventilation |

## Indoor activity ideas

Try community centre classes, mall walking, home workouts, stretching, light strength exercises, or dancing to music at home. If you use void-deck or indoor stair routines, go slowly, hold the handrail, and avoid blocking others. Stairs can become strenuous quickly, so keep the session short if you are not used to them.

For people who use step goals, indoor walking still counts. A few 10-minute blocks can replace one long outdoor walk on a hazy day.

## Who should take extra care

Older adults and people with heart or lung conditions should be more cautious when air quality is poor. Children and pregnant people may also need to reduce outdoor exertion. If you feel chest tightness, wheezing, unusual breathlessness, dizziness, or feel unwell, stop the activity and seek medical advice.

## Keep the routine flexible

Have a simple "hazy-day plan": one indoor walking route, one home strength routine, and one gentle stretching option. This keeps your habit going without forcing outdoor exercise when conditions are not suitable.

## Key points

- Check the PSI before heading out for outdoor exercise.
- Reduce prolonged strenuous outdoor activity when air quality is poor.
- Swap to indoor options such as community centre classes, mall walking, home workouts, or careful indoor stairs.
- Older adults and people with heart or lung conditions should take extra care.
- See a doctor if you feel unwell.

## When to talk to a health professional

Speak to a doctor if haze triggers breathing symptoms, chest discomfort, wheezing, dizziness, or if you have a heart or lung condition and are unsure what activity level is safe.
